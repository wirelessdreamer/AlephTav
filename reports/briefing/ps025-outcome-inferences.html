Psalm 25: inferred briefing answers

Bone and Ash · 45 questions

These suggestions describe the existing lyric and song setting. They are not recovered original answers, and they do not direct future translation until you adopt them. Confidence measures fit to the wording, not certainty about your intent.

1. Allocating the 6/8 form

medium

Keep 6/8 while allowing variable phrase and stanza lengths to retain parallel clauses and repetition.

The setting labels its intro "6/8 throughout" and uses stanzas of different lengths, a repeated chorus, and a repeated outro.

The written setting supports flexible phrasing, but it does not record bar counts or prove a decision about additional metrical units.

2. Divine names

high

Use LORD for YHWH and God for Elohim and its inflected forms.

The lyric repeatedly uses "LORD"; verses 2 and 22 use "God".

The visible divine-name distinction matches this option.

3. Heading and attribution

medium

Keep "Of David" as a distinct introductory line, without interpreting the attribution as "By David" or "For David".

Verse 1 prints "Of David." separately; it is the first line of the arrangement's intro.

The wording and position are clear. Whether the line is spoken, sung, or only printed cannot be established from these records.

4. Representing the acrostic

unresolved

Unresolved

The lyric and arrangement have no Hebrew letter labels.

The absence of labels does not establish whether an explanatory acrostic note was intended or supplied elsewhere.

5. Disturbed acrostic positions

high

Keep the transmitted clause order, with "My God" in verse 2 and both "See" openings in verses 18 and 19.

Verse 2 opens "My God"; verses 18 and 19 both begin "See".

These features favor retention over an acrostic reconstruction. An intention to add explanatory notes is not recoverable.

6. Human gender and number

high

Use a gender-inclusive singular such as ‘the one,’ with appropriate English pronouns.

Verse 12 has "the one" and "them"; verse 13 has "Their"; verse 22 has "all their troubles".

These are gender-inclusive singular and collective forms rather than representative "man/he/his".

7. Verb time

medium

Use contextual verb time: perfect for established trust, present for habitual divine action, and future for expected guidance.

Verse 2: "I've trusted"; verses 8–9: "he teaches" and "He guides"; verse 12: "He'll show them". The arrangement uses "I've waited" where the lyric uses "I wait".

The result is mixed across verses and between lyric and arrangement, so neither offered policy alone describes every instance.

8. The heart’s distress

high

Retain the report that the troubles of the heart have grown.

Verse 17: "The troubles in my heart have grown".

The first clause reports increasing trouble rather than asking for room.

9. The chooser of the way

high

Describe God teaching the worshipper which way to choose.

Verse 12: "He'll show them which path to choose"; the arrangement says "He'll teach them which way to choose".

God instructs the worshipper in choosing, rather than explicitly choosing the way for them.

10. Counsel, friendship, or strength

high

Render sod as confidential counsel or a shared secret.

Verse 14: "The LORD confides in those / who fear him".

Confiding expresses confidential counsel rather than Greek strength/support wording.

11. Protecting virtues or upright companions

high

Retain the Hebrew virtues as personified guardians and omit the additional divine address.

Verse 21: "Let integrity and uprightness / guard me; / I wait for you."

The guardians are virtues, with no added divine address or upright companions.

12. Assurance or prayer about shame

high

Make both halves declarations about who will and will not suffer shame.

Verse 3 says both "will be put to shame" and "None ... will be put to shame".

Both halves are declarations, not requests.

13. Guidance as statement or wish

high

State God’s habitual or expected guidance.

Verse 9: "He guides the humble in justice; / he teaches the humble his way."

The verse describes habitual guidance rather than asking for it.

14. Pardon requested or expected

high

Use a direct request for pardon.

Verse 11: "forgive my guilt".

This is a direct request.

15. Guarding requested or assured

high

Express a wish that integrity and uprightness guard the speaker.

Verse 21: "Let integrity and uprightness / guard me".

The guarding is a wish or petition, not a future assertion.

16. Loyal love and compassion

medium

Distinguish mercy/mercies from faithful love; retain faithful love in verses 6, 7, and 10. The setting keeps plural mercies and acts of faithful love in verse 6.

The lyric has "your mercy, / your faithful love"; the setting has "your mercies, / your acts of faithful love". Verses 7 and 10 retain "faithful love".

The distinction is clear, but the lyric and arrangement differ on plural/deed language, so no single offered option fully captures both.

17. Truth and faithfulness

high

Use truth in both places.

Verses 5 and 10 both use "truth".

The same English term recurs.

18. Soul, life, and the lifted self

high

Retain soul throughout and preserve lifting language where English permits.

Verses 1, 13, and 20 use "soul"; verse 1 has "lift up" and verse 18 has "lift away all my sins".

The wording retains soul and makes the lifting language audible.

19. Waiting and hoping

high

Use wait consistently and retain the day-long duration.

The lyric uses "wait" in verses 3, 5, and 21, with "all day" in verse 5; the setting uses "waited" in verses 5 and 21.

Waiting, rather than hoping, is the recurring lexical choice; tense varies.

20. Shame and failed trust

high

Retain the recurring shame/be-shamed wording in verses 2, 3, and 20.

The lyric has "don't let me be shamed", "will be put to shame", and "don't let me be shamed".

The output preserves shame language. It does not establish a separate psychological theory of what shame means.

21. Treacherous conduct and reqam

high

Describe treachery committed without cause or justification.

Verse 3: "Those who betray without cause".

The qualifier explicitly describes causeless betrayal.

22. Ways, paths, and instruction

high

Keep ways, paths, guiding, and teaching, allowing their meanings to develop through context.

Verses 4–5 and 8–12 retain "ways", "paths", "Guide", and "teach".

The road and instruction imagery remains visible.

23. Salvation and rescue

high

Use God my savior or describe God as the one who delivers me.

Verse 5: "You are the God who saves me".

The title is expressed as God's saving action rather than the abstract noun salvation.

24. Mercy from ancient times

high

Use from of old or since ancient times.

The setting has "for they're from long ago"; the lyric has "through the ages".

The setting supports ancient time. The lyric alone is less specific about ancient history versus eternity.

25. Remembering mercy and wrongdoing

high

Repeat remember and do not remember, leaving their practical force implicit.

Verse 6: "remember"; verse 7: "Don't remember" and "remember me".

Remembering is repeated without substituting explanatory actions or adding good deeds.

26. Wrongdoing or ignorance

high

Preserve distinctions such as sins, transgressions or rebellions, and iniquity or guilt.

Verse 7 has "sins" and "the times I rebelled"; verse 11 has "guilt"; verse 18 has "sins".

The output distinguishes wrongdoing terms and does not use Greek ignorance language.

27. The humble and the afflicted

high

Use humble.

Verse 9 repeats "the humble".

The lexical choice is explicit.

28. Justice and right conduct

high

Use in justice or in judgment, keeping the construction broad.

Verse 9: "He guides the humble in justice".

The broad "in justice" construction is retained.

29. Covenant and testimonies

high

Retain covenant while rendering testimonies as decrees or requirements.

Verse 10: "his covenant / and his commands"; verse 14 retains "covenant".

Covenant remains, while commands explains the requirements rather than retaining testimonies.

30. Keeping or seeking the covenant

high

Describe people who keep or observe the covenant and its stipulations.

Verse 10: "those who keep his covenant".

The output says keep, not seek.

31. The divine name as a reason

high

Retain for your name’s sake.

Verse 11: "for the sake of your name".

The name image remains without an explicit reputation paraphrase.

32. Because or although the guilt is great

medium

Present the greatness of the guilt as the urgent need for pardon, without adding "although".

Verse 11: "forgive my guilt; / there's so much to forgive".

The juxtaposition favors an appeal from need, but no explicit because/although connector settles the logical relation.

33. Fear of God

high

Retain fear in both verses.

Verses 12 and 14 retain "fears" and "fear".

Fear remains explicit rather than being replaced with loyalty language.

34. The rhetorical question

high

Keep the question and its answer-like continuation.

Verse 12: "Who is the one who fears the LORD?"

The question and following answer-like statement remain.

35. Dwelling in goodness

high

Use broader language of dwelling at ease or in well-being.

Verse 13: "Their soul will rest at ease".

The wording expresses broad ease rather than explicit material prosperity. It cannot establish whether an afterlife interpretation was intended.

36. Descendants and the land

high

Retain "seed" for descendants, but render the inheritance as "the land".

Verse 13: "their seed will inherit the land".

This combines features of different offered options; selecting any one option verbatim would misrepresent the outcome.

37. Who makes the covenant known

high

Make God the implied subject who reveals the covenant to the worshippers.

Verse 14: "he makes his covenant known"; the setting has "he shows them his covenant".

God is the subject who reveals the covenant.

38. Eyes, feet, and the snare

high

Retain eyes, feet, net, and refuge without explanatory additions.

Verse 15 retains "My eyes", "my feet", and "the net"; verse 20 has "I've taken shelter in you".

The bodily and shelter images stand without an added explanation of enemy entrapment.

39. Alone or an only child

high

Use alone, lonely, or desolate.

Verse 16: "I'm alone".

The output uses solitude rather than only-child wording.

40. Poor or afflicted

high

Use suffering language: pain, distress, and hardship, without making poverty or humiliation explicit.

Verse 16 has "in pain"; verse 17 has "distress"; verse 18 has "my pain and my hardship".

The actual equivalents express suffering; none of the offered phrasings exactly reproduces all these words.

41. Suffering and forgiveness

high

Keep the requests to see suffering and lift away sins side by side, without adding an explicit causal or transactional explanation.

Verse 18: "See my pain and my hardship; / lift away all my sins."

The clauses remain adjacent, with no explanation that suffering earns or causes forgiveness.

42. Numerous enemies and violent hatred

high

Describe many enemies and cruel or violent hatred, retaining the emphatic repetition where meter permits.

Verse 19: "See my enemies—so many, / so violent in their hate."

The output describes number and violent hatred, not specifically multiplication and injustice.

43. Whose integrity

high

Leave integrity and uprightness unpossessed.

Verse 21 names "integrity and uprightness" with no possessive.

Neither the speaker nor God is explicitly identified as the virtues' possessor.

44. The national ending

high

Give verse 22 a separate stanza or typographical break while retaining it in full.

The setting gives verse 22 a separate "Outro" section and repeats that outro.

The arrangement visibly separates the communal conclusion while retaining its words.

45. Redeeming Israel

high

Use redeem and let the context define the requested release.

Verse 22: "God, redeem Israel".

Redeem is retained explicitly.
